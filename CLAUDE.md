## What this app is

A web app that grades DECO field-training sessions. A manager uploads an audio recording of a training session; the app sends it to Google's Gemini API, which scores it against DECO's real training rubric and returns section-by-section scores, a total, a certified Yes/No, and written feedback. It also tracks where each field manager stands on their certification path.

**Live at:** https://development-dashboard.github.io

## Repo layout

- `index.html` — the entire front end. Single self-contained file: HTML, CSS, and JS inline. Eight tabs: **Dashboard** (certification roster), **Follow-Ups** (see below), plus one per session type — **Training Day**, **Team On-Site Day**, **Conversion Call**, **Stage 1 Interview**, **Reference Checks**, **Stage 2 Interview**. The Reference Checks tab covers two session types (candidate and parent) behind one picker — see its `variants` array.
  - **Follow-Ups (added 2026-08-13)** is the coaching queue: every graded session whose AI verdict came back as anything other than `Certified: Yes`, across all session types, in one filterable list that drains as people tick rows off. Jordan's ask, and the first piece of Phase 4 to actually exist. Design decisions worth not re-litigating:
    - **What pulls a session in is the AI's own `certified` value (column K), nothing re-derived.** So the list can never disagree with the grade on the card.
    - **The reason column splits three things a single "failed" label would flatten:** *scored under 6*, *never came up on the recording* (the audio-only blind spots — counts as 0 by design, not a performance problem), and *the AI wasn't sure* (`needs_human_review`). Keep them separate; that distinction is most of the tab's value.
    - **It's framed as coaching, not a verdict, and must stay that way** while the AI is uncalibrated — same rule as the rest of the app up to Phase 5.
    - **A person's region comes from the Roster, looked up by name** (`regionForPerson`). Nothing region-related was added to the Grading Log; a name that doesn't match the Roster shows as "Region not set" rather than disappearing from the filter.
    - **Someone already certified for that session type gets an "Already certified" flag** — a certified person slipping is a different situation from a trainee who was never expected to pass yet.
    - `fetchSubmissions()` is shared with Recent Submissions so there's one place that knows how sheet rows map onto sessions.
  - **Compare (added 2026-08-14)** is Phase 2 calibration on screen: pick a person and a session type and every AI grade they have lines up side by side, section by section, against a human grade typed in beside them. Jordan's ask, after V16 made it possible to grade one recording two different ways — *"see the difference between those two"* and then *"all 4 AI runs and the human comparison"*. He also said outright *"we just might not use it"*, and several decisions below follow from that rather than from the feature being load-bearing. Decisions worth not re-litigating:
    - **It handles N runs, never a fixed number.** The count is whatever happens to have been graded — two today, six next week — and a table built for four breaks on the first session that isn't. Verified at 2, 4 and 6.
    - **The human column is the REFERENCE, not a fourth opinion.** Phase 2 is "refine the prompt until the AI lines up with the district manager", so every figure here measures AI *against* human and never averages the two together.
    - **Audio vs transcript is read off column X, not guessed.** A session Code.gs wrote a transcript link for was transcript-graded; anything without one was graded from audio, which is also correct for every session predating V16. Gated through `audioLinkOf()` so a note typed into that column by hand can't be read as a transcript. It's a labelled chip on each column because that distinction is the entire question the tab was built to answer.
    - **A section with no confident score renders "never came up", never `0`.** It counts as 0 in the total by rubric rule, but drawing it as a zero turns "the audio never covered this" into "they did this badly" — the same distinction Follow-Ups exists to preserve. `compareScoreOf()` mirrors `followUpReasons()`'s test exactly and has to keep doing so.
    - **The human total is the SUM of what's typed, never separately entered.** Two sources for one figure is how they drift apart. A part-filled column says "3 of 11 filled in" rather than showing a total that reads like a grade.
    - **Deltas are signed.** "The AI is consistently generous" and "consistently harsh" are different findings, and a bare magnitude hides which one you're looking at.
    - **The typed human grades USED to live in `localStorage` and now live in the Grading Log at column Y — see "Saving the typed human grades" below.** The original note here said promoting them to a column was the upgrade if the tab earned its place; it did, and Jordan asked for it on 2026-08-14. ## Rules that matter when changing grading logic

- **BC only.** Everything is scoped to British Columbia. Other provinces are the final phase and explicitly not active work — don't build toward them.
- **Replacements 101 is always graded in BC**, in every territory, never marked N/A. (Confirmed by Jordan; a region-based alternative was considered and rejected.)
- **Certification formulas** must match the Smartsheets exactly:
  - Training Day: total out of 110 across 11 sections; certified = total ≥ 85 **and** every section ≥ 6.
  - Team On-Site Day: total out of 50 across 5 sections; **a recording passes** = total ≥ 38 **and** every section ≥ 6.
  - Conversion Call: total out of 80 across 8 categories; certified = total ≥ 48 **and** every section ≥ 6. **No Smartsheet to match here** — it's the flattened form of the source rubric's weighted 6.0/10, and 48 is both 60% of 80 and exactly 8 × the section pass mark. Certifies from a passing *manual* grade as of 2026-08-14; see the entry under Repo layout.
- **A Team On-Site Day certification takes THREE passing recordings, not one** (Jordan, 2026-08-14 — `SESSION_PASSES_REQUIRED`). The per-recording formula above is unchanged and still matches the Smartsheet; the count is a layer on top that the Smartsheet has no concept of, so **the Dashboard certifies LATER than the Smartsheet's own formula would**. That is the intended change, not a drift to reconcile. See the full entry under Repo layout.
- **A section with no confident score counts as 0**, not as "can't determine." `certified` is always a real Yes/No.
- **Consensus grading:** every session is graded by 3 sequential Gemini calls that get reconciled (median per section). This costs ~3x tokens and ~3x time versus one call — the tradeoff is deliberate, because single calls produced wildly inconsistent scores. Don't "optimize" it back to one call.
- **Passes that fail are survivable, but a degraded result must say so (V11).** A 429 or 5xx on one pass is retried (waiting the delay Google suggests, capped so backoff can't eat the 6-minute budget); a *daily* quota 429 is not retried, because waiting can't clear a daily cap. If a pass still fails, grading proceeds with whatever completed rather than throwing away passes already paid for — but the reviewer notes say how many actually ran, and **a single completed pass flags every section for human review**. That last part matters: `reconcileSection` can't detect disagreement it never saw, so one uncorroborated pass would otherwise produce a confident-looking score with nothing behind it.
- Because grading is 3 sequential calls, a long recording can approach Apps Script's 6-minute web app limit. If timeouts appear, either lower `GRADING_RUNS_PER_SESSION` to 2 or restructure as parallel `UrlFetchApp.fetchAll` calls.
- **Evidence timestamps (V10).** Every section returns `evidence_clips` — 1–3 `{at, quote}` moments pointing at the audio that drove its score — and the results card turns those into clickable jump points on an in-page player. The point is that a flagged section can be checked in about a minute instead of by re-listening to the whole session. These are an aid to a human's judgement, **not** citations. Don't present them as exact anywhere in the UI.
- **Measured timestamp accuracy — two independent measurements, both on the same short test clip.** The feature works; the open question is length, not accuracy (see below).
  - **2026-08-13, Training Day prompt.** Jordan hand-checked all 5 citations against the audio. Cited 0:07 / 0:28 / 0:43 / 0:55 / 1:43; the matching exchanges start at 0:11 / 0:32 / 0:43 / 0:58 / 1:43. **Off by 0–4 seconds, always slightly early, never late.**
  - **2026-08-14, Team On-Site Day prompt, same audio file** (`Test Audio for AI.m4a` — Jordan confirmed it's the same recording, so this is a genuine second reading of the same ground truth, through a *different* rubric and prompt). Against the anchors established the day before: the three pillars at 0:43 → **cited 0:45**; "go the extra mile" / the pickle line at 1:07 → **cited 1:06**; replacement criteria at 1:43 → **cited 1:42**; the VIP package at 2:06 → **cited 2:06 exactly**. **Off by 0–2 seconds, and this time in both directions rather than always early.**

Don't let "measured twice" get quoted as "validated".
- **HOW TO VERIFY A TIMESTAMP — read this before concluding they're broken.** Check a citation against **where that topic starts**, not against a specific phrase inside it. The prompt tells Gemini to point at where an exchange *begins*, so measuring against a line spoken later in the same exchange makes it look badly late when it isn't. This exact mistake was made on 2026-08-13: two citations looked ~23s off and were written up as an uncorrectable "clock slip", with an elaborate silence-in-the-audio theory attached. Don't rebuild that theory; measure properly instead.
- **Corrections are the fix-list, not a second Focus Areas (V13, 2026-08-13).** Jordan's point: a Training Day runs over two days and the recording gets submitted while there's still time left with the Site Operator, so the card was missing the one thing a trainer needs in that moment — what to go back over before the session ends. Each entry is `{section, issue, correct, at, priority}`: what was actually said or skipped, what it should be per the rubric/reference data, where to hear it, and `must_fix` vs `should_fix`. Things that matter:
  - **The prompt has to say explicitly that this is not `focus_areas`** (Code.gs rule 9). Focus areas are for the *next* session; corrections are for *this* one, before it's over. Without that line Gemini returns the same list twice.
  - **An entry with no timestamp is an omission, and those are the valuable ones** — something that never came up at all still needs covering before the day ends. The card labels those "never came up" / "Missing" instead of "Heard", rather than just showing no jump point.
  - **An unrecognised or missing `priority` renders with no badge**, not a guessed one. The page must not claim an urgency the AI never assigned.
  - **Half a correction is dropped, not shown.** An entry missing either the issue or the fix can't be acted on, and a to-do row nobody can act on is worse than one fewer row.
  - Capped at 6, `must_fix` first — it's a list somebody works through in the hours that are left, not a report.
- **`CLIP_PREROLL_SECONDS` is 3.** Citations run 0–4s early already, so playback only needs a few seconds of run-up. The original 10 was guesswork and just added dead air before the exchange. The ±15/+30 nudge buttons on the player stay useful regardless — the cited moment is the *start* of a topic, so the specific line a manager wants is usually a little further in.
- **Sections with more than one timestamp (2026-08-13).** A section can carry up to 3 moments, and three bare times (`00:34 02:15 07:41`) gave a manager nothing to choose between without hovering each one. So:
  - **One clip renders exactly as before** — a compact time chip, quote in the tooltip. **Two or more stack**, each with the first 5 words of its quote underneath. The split is deliberate: there's nothing to tell apart when there's only one, and a quote line on all 11 sections would undo the card's 2026-08-13 tightening. Measured at 1280px: a 3-clip section grows the row to ~294px, a 1-clip section stays at 160px.
  - **A moment two or more passes independently landed on says so in words, under the chip: "2 passes point here".** `mergeClips` always counted this (it's how it ranks when a section is over the 3-clip cap) and then dropped it before rendering, so a moment all 3 passes flagged looked identical to one a single pass mentioned. The count says *where to start listening is a better bet*, *not* that the score is more right — the footnote says so.
  - **This shipped first as a compact `×2` / `×3` badge on the chip and that was wrong.** Jordan read `1:42 ×2` on sight as "there are 2 timestamps to review here" — the exact opposite of its meaning, since a corroborated clip is ONE moment several passes agreed on. Any small glyph next to a number invites that reading. **Don't reintroduce a badge here; the count gets plain words or it doesn't ship.** The footnote now leads with the distinction (one moment vs. several) rather than just defining the marker.
  - **The count is never shown as a fraction.** A degraded grade (V11) can run on fewer than 3 passes, so a printed denominator would sometimes be a lie.
  - **This was front-end only — no `Code.gs` change, no redeploy.** Both copies of `mergeClips` already emit `runs`; only rendering changed. The 158px score column is why quotes are cut to 5 words with a 2-line CSS clamp behind them.
  - Still unaddressed: if more than 3 distinct moments survive merging, the extras are dropped with nothing on screen saying so.
- **Still untested at length.** All measurement so far — both hand-checks — is from the *same* short test clip. Whether accuracy holds across a 60-minute session is genuinely unknown — check one citation near the start and one near the end of the first real session, using the verification method above. If accuracy degrades badly over a long recording, the honest response is to stop rendering a clickable time and show only the quote, so a manager searches for words instead of trusting a number that lies.

## Known quirks worth knowing before you debug

- **Corrections have no sheet column, and that's deliberate.** The `corrections` fix-list (V13) rides along inside column S's raw JSON and gets rebuilt by `correctionsFromRuns()` in `index.html`, exactly like evidence clips. It has no column of its own because T/U/V now belong to the follow-up state — a 20th column appended by `Code.gs` would land on top of `Follow-Up Status`. Both sides take corrections from the **last completed run** (they're prose, not scores) and both sort by **priority only**, relying on a stable sort to preserve the AI's own ordering inside each priority. Change one side's ordering and a session's fix-list will reorder itself depending on whether it was freshly graded or re-opened from the log.
- **Column X ("Transcript Link") is Code.gs's, and it is the one extended column the front end does NOT write (V16).** It has to be the backend's: the backend is what knows where the transcript ended up in Drive. ## The final roadmap (2026-08-14) — THIS IS THE CURRENT ONE

- **8-HOUR RECORDINGS — V17, built and tested 2026-08-14, NOT YET DEPLOYED and NOT YET RUN ON A REAL RECORDING.** Jordan: *"can you make the code work for 8+ hour recordings."* Most of what was needed already existed — V16 built the `fromSeconds`/`toSeconds` plumbing and then never drove it. Decisions worth not re-litigating:
  - **THERE IS A HARD CEILING AT ~9h01m AND IT IS NOT OURS.** Gemini bills audio at 32 tokens/second and `gemini-3.5-flash` reads 1,048,576 input tokens per request, so 32,518 seconds is the absolute most that can reach the model in one piece. Google's own documented audio cap (9.5h) is *looser*, so this is the one that binds. **Past it a recording cannot be graded at all and must be split into two files submitted as two sessions** — and no windowing trick avoids that, because a window still sends the whole file. `MAX_AUDIO_SECONDS_PER_REQUEST` (Code.gs) and `MAX_GRADABLE_SECONDS` (index.html) are that number; one more duplicated constant on the pile. Jordan was asked and confirmed 8–9h is the real range, so multi-part upload was scoped OUT.
  - **Re-confirmed from Google's docs, because the whole design turns on it: there is still no way to bill part of an audio file.** Asking for a time range in the prompt ingests and charges for the entire file. So every window re-reads everything, and that cost is real and is in the estimate.
  - **The 6-minute wall is made of OUTPUT, not input — that is the insight the V16 note missed.** V16 assumed windows only help with the 65,536-token output cap and concluded they were pure loss. But an 8-hour transcript is ~63k output tokens in ONE execution, which is minutes of generation on top of reading a 1M-token file. A bounded window shrinks the generation, and each window gets its own fresh 6-minute budget. That is what makes 8 hours finishable.
  - **Windows are sized by OUTPUT TOKENS (30,000), never by clock time, and only used when needed.** `transcriptWindowCount()` returns the SMALLEST count that keeps each call safe, so a 2:22 clip and a 3-hour session still run as **one open-ended pass — byte-identical to V16** (verified). A 4h33m session is 2 windows, 8h is 3, 9h is 3. **If a window ever overruns 6 minutes, `TRANSCRIPT_WINDOW_TARGET_TOKENS` is the one number to turn down.** How fast the model writes is a guess until a real long session runs.
  - **The last window is sent open-ended (`toSeconds: 0`), never bounded by the duration the browser read.** A container that reports its length slightly short would otherwise stop the transcript a few seconds early with nothing downstream able to detect it. Running long costs nothing; stopping short loses evidence — the same asymmetry that governs the resume point.
  - **AUDIO NOW GOES FIRST IN A TRANSCRIPTION REQUEST, prompt second, and that is the cost story.** Implicit caching (default on for 2.5+ models) only matches on a shared PREFIX, so with the prompt leading it could never fire — the varying part came first. Audio first makes the ~1M identical tokens the prefix, and a hit bills at **$0.15/M against $1.50/M**. Never promised and never the headline figure, but it was impossible by construction before. **Deliberately NOT applied to the grading passes** in `buildGeminiRequestOptions`: those are three *identical* requests fired in parallel, so nothing is warm and the order buys nothing there, while changing it would disturb the short-clip path every measurement in this project rests on. Two tests pin the grading order in place.
  - **At 8 hours the transcript route COSTS MORE than listening ($5.10 vs $4.24) and is still correct.** Three windows re-reading the audio eventually outweigh three straight listens. It stays the right route because listening means three calls that each swallow the whole recording inside one 6-minute execution — the thing that cannot finish. **The plan box says this out loud** rather than quoting a saving it isn't making; the old copy would have read as nonsense next to its own numbers. The re-grade price ($0.38 vs $4.24) is untouched and is still the Phase 2 win.
  - **Two caps on the transcription loop, because it is the one loop in this app that spends money per iteration** (~$1.40 a pass at 8h whether it achieves anything or not). `TRANSCRIPT_MAX_ATTEMPTS_PER_WINDOW = 3` is the tight one and gives the useful error — it names the stretch that keeps stopping short — and `MAX_TRANSCRIBE_CALLS = 12` is the backstop. Both are checked BEFORE the call, never after: by the time a pass answers it has already been charged for. Measured: a pathological model that never finishes now costs 3 passes (~$4) instead of 12 (~$16), and **grades nothing**.
  - **`joinTranscriptChunks()` drops verbatim repeats at a window boundary.** A resumed pass restarts ON its last timestamp (early beats late), so that line genuinely arrives twice — and a grader cannot tell a stitching artefact from a trainer repeating themselves, which the delivery rubric scores. Exact matches only, tail only, capped, so the worst case is a duplicate surviving rather than real session being eaten.
  - **The progress bar got a ratchet, and it was a real bug, not a precaution.** A window that truncates and resumes calls `setPhase` without advancing the window index, which walked the bar backwards — on precisely the long recordings where somebody is already watching it nervously. Clamped in `renderProgress` against a high-water mark so every future call site is covered too.

    - **THERE IS NO WAY TO SALVAGE A PASS AT 5:59, and the reason is worth keeping written down because it is the obvious idea.** Writing the transcript is ONE blocking `UrlFetchApp` call. Gemini does not stream it back; the whole answer arrives at the end or not at all. At 5:59 the script is holding **nothing**, so "saving what it has" would write an empty file. A killed pass loses its own work and its own cost, never the session — every finished pass is saved to Drive before the next starts.
    - **A pre-flight "is there time left?" check would also be pointless here**, and was considered and dropped: each transcription pass is its own POST, so it is its own execution with its own fresh 6 minutes and the Gemini call is essentially all of it.     - **270s deliberately, not tighter.** Every shrink means more passes and every pass re-reads the whole recording, so over-reacting to a pass that was comfortably fine is itself expensive.
